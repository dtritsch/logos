# Logic, Rhetoric, and Applied Intellect
### Master Curriculum & Roadmap

## Overview of Program Phases

* **Phase 1:** Formal Logic, Propositional Calculus & Cognitive Heuristics
* **Phase 2:** Historical Survey of Rhetorical Tradition
* **Phase 3:** Classical Antiquity (Plato, Aristotle, Cicero, Quintilian, Augustine)
* **Phase 4:** Enlightenment & Philosophy of Language (Bacon, Locke, Campbell)
* **Phase 5:** 20th-Century Revival (Burke, Perelman, Toulmin, Richards)
* **Phase 6:** Modern Critical Discourse & Cognitive Metaphor (Lakoff, Foucault, Jasinski)
* **Phase 7:** Multidisciplinary Applied Systems (Legal Reasoning, Game Theory, Crisis PR)
* **Phase 8:** Capstone Doctoral-Level Treatise & Logic Mapping

---

## PHASE 1: The Internal Engine (Logic, Grammar, & Mental Mechanics)

*Goal: Build non-negotiable logical hygiene, structural validity, and cognitive self-awareness before touching complex texts.*

### Core Modules & Primary Readings:

1. **Formal Deductive Logic & Propositional Calculus**
* *Concepts:* Truth tables, categorical syllogisms, predicate logic, sound vs. valid arguments.
* *Anchor Text:* *A Concise Introduction to Logic* (Patrick J. Hurley).

2. **Grammar, Syntactic Architecture, & Prose Rhythm**
* *Concepts:* Sentence mechanics, subordination, coordination, clarity under complexity.
* *Anchor Text:* *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace* (Joseph M. Williams).

3. **Informal Logic & Fallacy Detection**
* *Concepts:* The 20+ major informal fallacies, structural leaps, ambiguity, begging the question.
* *Anchor Text:* *Attacking Faulty Reasoning* (T. Edward Damer).

4. **Cognitive Psychology & Heuristics**
* *Concepts:* System 1 vs. System 2 thinking, confirmation bias, cognitive load, framing effects.
* *Anchor Text:* *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Daniel Kahneman).

* **Milestone Deliverable:** Formal Logic Exam (completing all diagnostic proofs in Hurley) + a 10-page error audit analyzing 10 real-world policy documents for structural/informal fallacies.

---

## PHASE 2: Comprehensive Survey & Epistemology

*Goal: Construct the master map of human knowledge and trace how rhetorical theory developed over 2,500 years.*

### Core Modules & Primary Readings:

1. **Epistemology & Theory of Knowledge**
* *Concepts:* Justified true belief, skepticism, Bayesian updating, perception, and justification.
* *Anchor Text:* *Epistemology: A Contemporary Introduction* (Robert Audi).

2. **The Comprehensive Historical Survey of Rhetoric**
* *Concepts:* The chronological evolution of rhetoric from ancient speech to modern media.
* *Anchor Text:* *The Rhetorical Tradition: Readings from Classical Times to the Present* (Patricia Bizzell & Bruce Herzberg) [~1,600 pages].

3. **Narrative History of Rhetoric**
* *Concepts:* Bridging the historical gaps between the Greek, Roman, Medieval, Renaissance, and Modern eras.
* *Anchor Text:* *Rhetoric in the European Tradition* (Thomas O. Conley).

* **Milestone Deliverable:** Annotated Bibliography entry (300 words each) for the first 15 major historical figures in Bizzell & Herzberg.

---

## PHASE 3: Classical Antiquity to the Renaissance (The Codification of Art)

*Goal: Master primary source texts where rhetoric was codified as a formal discipline (*techne*), political tool, and moral imperative.*

### Primary Text Reading List:

1. **Plato:** *Gorgias* & *Phaedrus* (False persuasion vs. philosophical truth).
2. **Aristotle:** *On Rhetoric* (Trans. George A. Kennedy) (The Rhetorical Triangle, *Ethos/Pathos/Logos*, genres, and enthymemes).
3. **Cicero:** *De Oratore* (The 5 Canons: Invention, Arrangement, Style, Memory, Delivery; the ideal orator).
4. **Quintilian:** *Institutio Oratoria* (Books 1 & 10–12) (Moral communication: "The good man speaking well").
5. **St. Augustine:** *On Christian Doctrine* (Book IV) (Pagan rhetoric adapted for homiletics and biblical interpretation).

* **Milestone Deliverable:** A 2,500-word synthesis essay answering: *"How did the concept of the Enthymeme and hidden premises evolve from Aristotle's analytics through Cicero's civic oratory?"*

---

## PHASE 4: Enlightenment, Hermeneutics, & Philosophy of Language

*Goal: Understand how the Scientific Revolution and modern philosophy reshaped human communication, meaning, and interpretation.*

### Primary Text Reading List & Modules:

1. **Enlightenment Rhetoric:** Francis Bacon (*Novum Organum* selections), George Campbell (*Philosophy of Rhetoric*), and Hugh Blair.
2. **Philosophy of Language:**
* *Concepts:* Semantics vs. pragmatics, speech act theory, sense and reference.
* *Anchor Readings:* Gottlob Frege, J.L. Austin (*How to Do Things With Words*), and John Searle.

3. **Hermeneutics & Interpretation Theory:**
* *Concepts:* Textual interpretation, context, and the "hermeneutic circle."
* *Anchor Readings:* Hans-Georg Gadamer (*Truth and Method* selections).


* **Milestone Deliverable:** A formal critique translating dense philosophical prose into symbolic logic notation while analyzing its rhetorical framing.

---

## PHASE 5: The 20th Century Revival (The "New Rhetoric")

*Goal: Study how theorists reinvented rhetoric to explain mass propaganda, psychological warfare, and structural power.*

### Primary Text Reading List:

1. **Kenneth Burke:** *A Rhetoric of Motives* & *Language as Symbolic Action* (Dramatism, terministic screens, identification).
2. **Chaïm Perelman & Lucie Olbrechts-Tyteca:** *The New Rhetoric: A Treatise on Argumentation* (Moving past formal logic; universal vs. particular audiences).
3. **Stephen Toulmin:** *The Uses of Argument* (The Toulmin Model: Claim, Data, Warrant, Backing, Rebuttal, Qualifier).
4. **I.A. Richards:** *The Philosophy of Rhetoric* (Rhetoric as the study of misunderstanding; semantic triangle).
5. **Lloyd Bitzer:** *"The Rhetorical Situation"* (1968) (Exigence, Audience, Constraints).

* **Milestone Deliverable:** Perform a complete Toulmin and Burkeian analysis on a landmark Supreme Court decision or historic speech.

---

## PHASE 6: Modern Critical, Visual, & Cognitive Rhetoric

*Goal: Examine how contemporary language, technology, media, and metaphors shape human consciousness and social reality.*

### Primary Text Reading List & Modules:

1. **George Lakoff & Mark Johnson:** *Metaphors We Live By* (Metaphor as the fundamental cognitive structure of thought).
2. **Walter J. Ong:** *Orality and Literacy* (How print culture and literacy restructured human consciousness).
3. **Constitutive Rhetoric & Discourse:** James Jasinski (*Sourcebook on Rhetoric*) and Michel Foucault (*The Order of Things* / *The Archaeology of Knowledge* selections).
4. **Visual & Digital Rhetoric:**
* *Concepts:* Non-verbal argument, document design, visual tropes, media ecology, digital framing.


* **Milestone Deliverable:** A 3,000-word critical monograph analyzing a modern public controversy or media campaign using Jasinski's constitutive framework and Lakoff's cognitive metaphor theory.

---

## PHASE 7: Multidisciplinary Applied Systems

*Goal: Take your synthesized engine of logic and rhetoric and deploy it across high-stakes, real-world fields.*

### Core Modules & Applied Fields:

1. **Legal Reasoning & Judicial Rhetoric:**
* *Concepts:* Case law analysis, statutory interpretation, *stare decisis*, appellate briefs.
* *Anchor Text:* *Making Your Case: The Art of Persuading Judges* (Antonin Scalia & Bryan A. Garner).

2. **Strategic Reasoning & Game Theory:**
* *Concepts:* Mathematical and rhetorical modeling of decision-making under conflict and incentives.
* *Anchor Text:* *Games, Strategies, and Decision Making* (Joseph E. Harrington).

3. **Rhetoric of Negotiation & Conflict Resolution:**
* *Concepts:* Principled negotiation, interest-based bargaining, reframing impasse.
* *Anchor Text:* *Getting to Yes* (Fisher, Ury, & Patton).

4. **Propaganda & Mass Persuasion:**
* *Concepts:* Manufacturing consent, media framing, institutional rhetoric.
* *Anchor Text:* *Manufacturing Consent* (Chomsky & Herman) / *Propaganda* (Edward Bernays).

* **Milestone Deliverable:** A complete Legal Brief and Game-Theoretic Strategy Paper for a complex, real-world regulatory or commercial dispute.

---

## PHASE 8: Doctoral Capstone & Comprehensive Synthesis

*Goal: Achieve full mastery by producing original, publication-quality intellectual work.*

### Capstone Requirements:

1. **The Comprehensive Oral/Written Defense (Self-Exam):** Answer 3 broad, high-level prompts integrating the entire reading list (e.g., *"Compare Aristotle's concept of Logos with Toulmin's Warrant and Perelman's Universal Audience, evaluating their efficacy in legal argumentation"*).
2. **The Master Monograph:** Produce a **40–60 page original treatise** that takes a complex, multi-variable real-world problem (philosophical, technical, or political) and provides:
* A formal symbolic logic map verifying thesis validity.
* A complete classical rhetorical arrangement (*Exordium* to *Peroratio*).
* A rigorous critique of opposing logical fallacies.
* Application of cognitive, game-theoretic, and audience-specific framing.
